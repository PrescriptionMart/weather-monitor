How this is decided
  • We read the National Weather Service forecast for 11am–6pm on the delivery day, local time at the address. That's when next-day air arrives, plus about 3 hours on the porch.
  • We check 2,750 places across the US: at least one for every group of ZIPs starting with the same 3 digits, and more in the mountains.
  • All ZIPs starting with the same 3 digits need a winter pack when any place among them stays 32°F (freezing) or colder for 4 hours in a row in that window. A shorter dip to freezing doesn't count. When more than half of a state's 3-digit groups need one, the whole state does.
  • The 4-hour low shown on the map and in the label check is the warmest it gets during a place's coldest 4 hours in a row. 32°F or below means winter pack.
  • If part of a group didn't load, it shows as Not checked — ask a pharmacist, unless the rest of the group's 4-hour low is well above freezing (over 52°F) at about the same elevation. Nothing unchecked is ever shown as standard.
  • Ship nights: Monday–Thursday deliver the next day. Friday is for Saturday-delivery items. Carrier holidays push delivery to the next service day.
  • The forecast is saved on this computer and updates itself every hour. The first load of the day takes two or three minutes; after that it opens instantly.
